When Claude Code sends 33,000 tokens before you even type a word, most engineers call it wasteful. They compare it to OpenCode’s 7,000 and assume Anthropic made a poor engineering tradeoff. They’re wrong. This isn’t inefficiency. It’s a moat. The 33K isn’t noise. It’s scaffolding. System prompts. Tool schemas. Repository instructions. Subagent bootstraps. Every single request carries the weight of your team’s entire context, your AGENTS.md file, your MCP servers, your custom policies. And that’s the point. OpenCode optimizes for low per-request cost. Claude Code optimizes for high switching cost. The difference isn’t technical, it’s strategic. In a world where AI coding agents are becoming the default, the real competition isn’t who writes cleaner code faster. It’s who becomes the irreversible interpreter of your codebase. Claude Code doesn’t want to be a tool. It wants to be the nervous system. Every time you use it, you’re not just asking for a suggestion. You’re reinforcing a dependency. The system prompt isn’t just metadata, it’s your team’s institutional knowledge encoded into a stateful session. The 72KB AGENTS.md file? That’s your org’s coding standards, security rules, and legacy patterns, now baked into every API call. You can’t delete it without breaking the workflow. This isn’t accidental. It’s designed. The cache inefficiency? That’s intentional. OpenCode’s prefix is byte-identical across sessions. Claude Code rewrites tens of thousands of tokens every time. Why? Because cache reuse would make switching easier. By forcing the system to rehydrate context on every turn, Anthropic ensures that your team’s workflow is tied to the live state of the agent. You’re not caching a payload, you’re caching a relationship. And when you add subagents? The cost explodes. A simple task that costs 121,000 tokens becomes 513,000 when split across two agents. Each subagent carries its own bootstrap cost. The parent consumes the transcript. The system is designed to make decomposition expensive, because complexity is sticky. This isn’t about price per token. It’s about the invisible tax of institutional adoption. Once your team builds workflows around Claude Code’s persistent context, you can’t just switch to another agent. You’d need to rebuild every instruction file. Reconfigure every policy. Re-encode your team’s tacit knowledge into a new system. That’s not a migration. It’s a rewrite. Meanwhile, developers are screaming for control. A recent study of 56 professional engineers found that 72.6% of configuration options were rated as useful, but only a third were even known to users. They want granular control over confidence thresholds, response length, and suggestion visibility. Yet Claude Code’s design assumes the opposite: that the system should manage context, not the developer. This tension reveals the real product bet. Anthropic isn’t competing on accuracy or speed. It’s competing on embedding. It’s betting that enterprises will tolerate higher costs in exchange for a single, authoritative agent that understands their codebase better than any human can document. Think of it like Microsoft Office in the early 2000s. The file formats were bloated. The startup time was slow. But once you built a library of templates, macros, and styles, switching to Google Docs wasn’t just inconvenient, it was catastrophic. Claude Code is doing the same with code. The EU AI Act’s Article 12 demands transparency in AI behavior. Most companies will panic when they see the token logs. But Anthropic already knew they’d be asked. That’s why the logs are so detailed. They’re not a liability. They’re a compliance feature. You can’t audit what you don’t log. And if you’re logging everything, you’re already locked in. This isn’t about who’s better at coding. It’s about who owns the context. The next time someone says Claude Code is inefficient, ask them this: What would it cost to rebuild your team’s entire AI workflow from scratch? Not just the code. The culture. The rituals. The unspoken rules now embedded in every 85,000-token request. That’s the real cost of AI. Not tokens. Trust. The winner won’t be the fastest agent. It’ll be the one you can’t imagine working without.
