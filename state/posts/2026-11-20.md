## The experiment that flipped the narrative

I watched a KPMG pilot where more than 100 AI agents dissected the accounts of a FTSE 250 company and, in several instances, asked sharper, risk‑focused questions than the human audit team. The project, codenamed Project Exponential, placed the agents alongside a conventional audit crew, letting them collect data, analyse it, draft observations and suggest next steps. Cath Burnet, KPMG UK head of audit, said the agents went considerably deeper into issues, flagging areas that human auditors had not raised. The Financial Times reported that the trial was not a formally approved statutory audit, but an experimental exercise signed off by an audit partner.

## Why the static workflow is already obsolete

Traditional audits rely on junior accountants performing repetitive data‑gathering while senior partners supervise. This linear pipeline creates a bottleneck at the data‑entry stage.

In the KPMG trial the agents divided responsibilities, coordinated their investigations and produced draft conclusions without human prompting. When one agent mismatched a regulatory requirement, a human had to intervene, proving that oversight remains essential but also that the bottleneck is shifting from data entry to exception handling.

Data quality proved to be the biggest obstacle; firms without clean, structured information struggled to keep the agents productive. The experiment highlighted that clean, structured data is a prerequisite for autonomous audit agents.

## Product implications and the next‑generation compliance stack

If an autonomous agent can surface a hidden revenue‑recognition risk before a senior auditor even looks at the ledger, the value proposition of a compliance platform changes dramatically. Instead of a static checklist that a user fills out, the product becomes an interactive AI conversation that probes, follows up and escalates in real time.

The open‑source Mastra Factory illustrates how such orchestration can be built: it runs durable agents for long‑running sessions, stores conversational memory, pulls signals from PR comments and operates through a Slack‑driven kanban board. Last week the factory opened and merged 238 pull requests while humans merged 630, showing that human‑in‑the‑loop scaling is feasible.

Product teams should stop treating audits as a linear pipeline and start designing a dialogue engine where agents ask, listen and iterate. A practical roadmap looks like this: embed durable agents that can fetch documents, run rule‑based checks and surface anomalies; add a conversational layer that lets the agent propose follow‑up questions and capture human responses; use a knowledge base (like Mastra’s markdown wiki) to ground the agent in company‑specific policies and regulatory texts; implement a human‑in‑the‑loop guardrail that triggers when the agent’s confidence falls below a threshold or when regulatory mapping fails.

## Regulatory reality check

The UK Financial Reporting Council warned that AI must not erode auditor independence, integrity or professional scepticism. Over‑reliance on automated conclusions could invite data‑security breaches or fabricated information, and the ultimate audit opinion still rests with a human.

KPMG acknowledges that the technology is not yet mature enough for thousands of engagements, but plans to expand to as many as 20 larger audits. Whether the time savings translate into lower fees remains an open question, but the pressure on the Big Four business model is undeniable.

## Actionable steps for product leaders

First, audit your own data pipelines: identify where unstructured or dirty data blocks an agent from operating. Second, prototype a single‑question loop on a low‑risk reporting task and measure how often the AI uncovers a new risk signal.

Third, map the regulatory requirements that caused the human intervention in KPMG’s trial and encode them as test cases for your guardrails. If the prototype surfaces a previously missed issue, you have a concrete proof point to rally engineering and sales around an AI‑first compliance product.

The long view is that autonomous LLMs can do more than crunch numbers; they can drive the investigative dialogue that underpins audit quality. Builders who cling to static workflows will watch their relevance fade as platforms that embed self‑directed agents capture the high‑value, low‑cost segment of compliance work.
